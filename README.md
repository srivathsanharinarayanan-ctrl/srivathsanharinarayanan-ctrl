## Research Themes

### Stellar systems and observational geometry

I am interested in how the orientation of a stellar system can influence the signals we observe. A useful analysis has to separate the geometry of an observation from the physical properties being inferred, and make its assumptions visible rather than hiding them inside a model.

### Exoplanets and habitability catalogs

Catalogs are useful only when their source data, selection criteria, and calculated parameters are understandable. I am interested in how public astronomical datasets can be organized into a form that makes candidate comparison easier without confusing a catalog score with a confirmed discovery.

### Variable stars and astronomical transients

Time-dependent astronomical signals can be challenging to classify. I am interested in the process of checking positions, comparing observations with existing records, and being cautious about treating a candidate as a new object before the evidence supports that conclusion.

### Reproducible scientific tools

A result becomes more useful when other people can follow how it was produced. I want my projects to make data sources, transformations, assumptions, and limitations easier to inspect.

## How I Approach a Research Problem

<table>
  <tr>
    <th align="center" width="12%">Stage</th>
    <th width="28%">Question</th>
    <th>What I aim to make clear</th>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td align="center"><strong>01</strong></td>
    <td>Define the question</td>
    <td>What is being investigated, and what would count as useful evidence?</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td align="center"><strong>02</strong></td>
    <td>Locate the data</td>
    <td>Where the data comes from, what each field means, and which limitations matter.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td align="center"><strong>03</strong></td>
    <td>Prepare and inspect</td>
    <td>How missing values, units, outliers, and data quality are handled.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td align="center"><strong>04</strong></td>
    <td>Analyze or model</td>
    <td>Which method is used, which assumptions it requires, and how sensitive the result is.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td align="center"><strong>05</strong></td>
    <td>Check the result</td>
    <td>Whether the interpretation is consistent with the data and alternative explanations.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td align="center"><strong>06</strong></td>
    <td>Document the work</td>
    <td>How another person can inspect the code, understand the workflow, and reproduce the analysis.</td>
  </tr>
</table>

This is the standard I want to keep moving toward: useful analysis, explicit assumptions, and documentation that helps someone else continue the work.

## What I Want to Build Next

The projects I find most exciting are not just demonstrations. They are tools that help someone ask a better question, inspect the evidence, and get useful work done with less friction.

- **Astronomy utilities** that help organize observations, compare catalog entries, and support repeatable analysis.
- **Local AI research tools** that can search notes, summarize relevant material, and assist with structured workflows while keeping control of the underlying files and data.
- **Small, focused developer tools** that solve a real problem instead of adding complexity for its own sake.
- **Clearer open-source projects** with setup instructions, meaningful examples, documented limitations, and reproducible results.

I am especially interested in the point where astrophysics, data, and AI can support each other without obscuring the underlying science.

I have worked with many professors on astronomy and research-related topics, and I am keen to keep learning from people who approach scientific questions carefully.
